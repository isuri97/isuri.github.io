About me

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate at the University of Cambridge. As part of the Humanities and AI Virtual Institute (HAVI), I build AI-based methods for archival research — tools that make fragile and sensitive historical collections searchable, structured, and interpretable.

I received my PhD in Computing from Lancaster University, supervised by Prof Ruslan Mitkov and Prof Paul Rayson, where I applied natural language processing to Holocaust survivor testimonies.

Research focus. My work spans named entity recognition, knowledge graph construction, and retrieval over sensitive historical archives, particularly Holocaust testimony collections.

Background. Before my PhD, I worked as a Software Engineer at WSO2 and was a recipient of a Google Summer of Code award. I have also served as Senior Editorial Assistant for the Journal of Natural Language Engineering, Cambridge University Press.

Research

My research sits at the intersection of NLP, knowledge representation, and the digital humanities, with a focus on sensitive historical archives. Current strands include domain-adapted language models for named entity recognition over testimony corpora, incorporating differential privacy; knowledge graph construction pipelines linking people, places, and events across testimony collections; retrieval-augmented methods for archival search, including a linear-adapter RAG approach and cross-cultural analogical retrieval over narrative corpora; and the ethics of applying large language models to sensitive historical material.

I collaborate with EHRI, CLARIN-ERIC, the Wiener Holocaust Library, USHMM, and Yale's Fortunoff Video Archive.
